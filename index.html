The science

Why it works.

None of this is a gimmick. Each part of the diary leans on published research into stress, attention and mental health.

1Colouring and stress

Ten minutes of colouring lowers anxiety.

In a controlled study, adults who coloured a structured pattern for a short session reported significantly lower anxiety than those who drew freely (Curry and Kasser, 2005). A later trial found that a week of daily colouring reduced symptoms of anxiety and depression in adults (Flett et al., 2017). The panels in every diary are built for exactly that: structured, repetitive, no screen.

2Priorities and mental load

Write the plan, quiet the mind.

Unfinished tasks keep nagging at your attention, a pattern psychologists call the Zeigarnik effect. Research shows that simply writing a concrete plan for those tasks switches the nagging off, even before you do them (Masicampo and Baumeister, 2011). When the week is too big, the spread asks for three big hitters, the priorities with the most impact, and one day at a time.

3Tracking stress

You can only manage what you can see.

Putting feelings into words and onto paper is linked with better wellbeing and fewer intrusive thoughts (Pennebaker and Beall, 1986, and decades of expressive-writing studies since). The green-to-red score takes two seconds a day and gives you thirteen weeks of data at every quarterly check-in: your stress levels as a pattern, not a feeling.

Fifty Two Press diaries are a planning and wellbeing tool, not a medical treatment. If your stress or mental health is affecting your life, talk to a doctor or a qualified professional.

The book inside is the same in all four. Pick by the job. Executive: you carry the whole operation. Manager: you run a department or project. Team Leader: your first step into leading people. Professional: serious work, no team to hide behind.

Is it dated?

No. All 52 weeks are undated, so you can start any Monday and skip nothing if you take a week off.

Do I need to be good at colouring?

No. The panels are simple line work. Ten minutes with any pen is the point, not the result. The research on colouring and anxiety used ordinary pencils and structured patterns, which is what these are.

Does colouring really reduce stress?

Studies with adults have found that short sessions of structured colouring lower reported anxiety, and that a week of daily colouring reduced anxiety and depressive symptoms. It is not a treatment, but it is a reliable way to bring the dial down at the end of a hard day.
